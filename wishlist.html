Give · Equipment wishlist

Tools for student research.

Our students turn their questions into measurements, such as whether a lawn runs hotter than a garden, whether a creek carries more dissolved salt than tap water, or which plants get eaten the most. Simple instruments like the ones below let a high school student answer questions like these with real data, and most of them last for years.

New or gently used equipment is welcome, and so is surplus from a lab or classroom. To donate an item, email Grey Monroe and we will arrange the details.

Field instruments

  • Infrared thermometers. Point-and-read surface temperature, for comparing pavement, grass, and shade.
  • Temperature and humidity data loggers. Left in place for days, they record how conditions change under trees, in the open, or in a greenhouse.
  • Probe thermometers. For soil and water temperature.
  • Soil moisture meters. For comparing irrigated lawns, native plantings, and bare ground.
  • pH meters (with calibration solutions). For testing soil and water.
  • Conductivity (TDS/EC) meters. A measure of dissolved salts, for comparing creek, pond, and tap water.
  • Light meters and handheld anemometers (wind meters).
  • Rain gauges.
  • Trail cameras. For recording wildlife at night.
  • BBC micro:bit boards. Small programmable boards with built-in sensors, for students who want to build their own instruments.

For looking closely and measuring

  • Hand lenses. Enough for every student to have one.
  • Clip-on macro lenses for phones. Close-up photos for identifying species and analyzing images.
  • Foldscopes (folding paper microscopes). For pond water, pollen, and leaf cells.
  • Digital scales (0.01 g) and digital calipers. For weighing and measuring seeds, leaves, and samples.

Sampling gear

  • Measuring tapes (50 m) and quadrats (square sampling frames). For counting plants and species in the field.
  • Insect sweep nets and aquatic D-nets. For sampling insects and stream invertebrates, a standard way to judge the health of a creek.
  • Waterproof field notebooks.

Supplies we use up

  • Water test strips and nitrate and phosphate test kits. For questions about farm runoff and water quality.
  • Soil test kits (pH and nutrients).
  • Petri dishes and agar. For growing microbes from swabs of soil, plants, and surfaces.
  • Sticky insect traps.
  • Seeds and potting soil. For germination and plant growth experiments.
  • Sample bags, vials, marker flags, flagging tape, and permanent markers.
  • Mason jars. For simple soil texture tests.

Questions
