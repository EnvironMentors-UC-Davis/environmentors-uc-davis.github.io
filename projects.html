What makes a good project

You care about it.

Pick a topic you have a personal connection to, or a scientific question you actually want answered. You will spend months on it.

It is fun to do.

You should enjoy collecting the data. If it sounds like a fun thing to go do, that is a good sign.

How a project fits together

Every project follows the same steps. The example below is one way a soil question could go.

  1. ThemeSomething you care about. Healthy soil for growing food.
  2. QuestionWhat do you want to know? Does adding compost help bean seedlings grow?
  3. DesignAn experiment, or a plan for collecting data, that can answer the question. Grow beans in pots with and without compost, same light and water.
  4. HypothesisYour idea about how things work, something you can test with data. Compost adds nutrients that seedlings use to grow.
  5. Prediction“If my hypothesis is true, this is what I will see.” Beans with compost will be taller after four weeks.
  6. Do itRun the experiment and collect your data. Measure every plant once a week and record it.
  7. Show itEnd with a graph and a description of what you did and what you found. Plant height over time, with and without compost.

Apps for collecting data

Free apps that turn a phone or laptop into a research instrument. Some ask you to make a free account.

Record your data

  • Google Sheets A spreadsheet for your measurements, on your phone or laptop. One row per sample, one column per measurement.
  • Google Forms Make a quick form and fill it in each time you collect data. Answers land in a spreadsheet for you.

Identify what you find

  • iNaturalist Plant and animal ID from a photo, with location. Map one species across Woodland.
  • Merlin Bird ID Bird ID by photo or song. Cornell Lab. Which birds visit at dawn vs. dusk?

Phone as an instrument

  • Phyphox Phone sensors: light, sound, motion, pressure. Exports data. Is it cooler and darker under trees?
  • NIOSH Sound Level Meter Noise meter from the CDC. iPhone only. Traffic noise vs. bird diversity.

Measure from photos

  • LeafByte Leaf area and bite damage from a phone photo. Which plants get eaten most?
  • Canopeo Percent green cover from a photo. Drought stress in lawns vs. gardens.
  • Fiji (ImageJ) Measure length and area, count objects. Desktop. Seed counts, root length, leaf size.

Soil

  • LandPKS Soil texture by feel and land cover, on your phone. Garden vs. field vs. roadside soil.

Make your graph

  • CODAP Drag-and-drop graphs in the browser. No code. Turn your spreadsheet into a figure.
